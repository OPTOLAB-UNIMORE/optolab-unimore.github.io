Research

Five areas the laboratory publishes in: ophthalmic instrumentation and vision science, clinical and physiological optical measurement, spectral and radiometric measurement, automotive optical sensing, and nanoscale biosensing.

OptoLAB designs, develops and characterizes optical and optoelectronic measurement systems, combining experimental prototyping with modelling, simulation and signal processing across the complete measurement chain. The five areas below are the ones the laboratory actually publishes in; each lists a few representative results, and the full record is on the Publications page.

Ophthalmic instrumentation and vision science

The laboratory’s largest strand, spanning the instruments built for the eye and the vision science that motivates them. Work here covers pupillometry with selective stimulation of fundus photoreceptors, multi-wavelength and Maxwellian-view stimulators, automated assessment of the anterior chamber angle from ocular images, eye-state and eye-position classification for measurement systems, and eye, head and gaze tracking — through to binocular vision, eye posture in head-mounted displays and the environmental risk factors behind myopia progression in children.

Selected work

  • Assessment of a Vision-Based Technique for an Automatic Van Herick Measurement System — T. Fedullo et al., IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement, 2022.
  • A Child-Friendly Wearable Device for Quantifying Environmental Risk Factors for Myopia — A. Gibaldi, E. Harb, C. F. Wildsoet, M. S. Banks, Translational Vision Science & Technology, 2024.
  • Eye posture and screen alignment with simulated see-through head-mounted displays — A. Gibaldi et al., Journal of Vision, 2025.
  • PopEYE — Infrared Ocular Image Dataset for Eye State and Gaze-Direction Classification — G. Gibertoni, G. Borghi, L. Rovati, Zenodo, 2026.

Optical measurement for clinical and physiological monitoring

Instruments that measure what is happening in a patient, usually in real time and often in line with a clinical circuit. Recurring subjects are optical blood-gas and blood-pH sensing during extracorporeal circulation, haemoglobin and haematocrit measurement on dialysis machines, absorption and endogenous-fluorescence monitoring of dialysis treatment, and wearable physiological measurement such as electrodermal activity. Temperature compensation, biocompatibility and validation against reference laboratory analysis are treated as part of the measurement problem rather than as afterthoughts.

Selected work

  • Validation of Hemoglobin and Hematocrit Measurements from a Dialysis Machine Sensor Compared to Laboratory Analysis — N. Morisi et al., Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2025.
  • Optical sensing for real-time blood monitoring in extracorporeal circulation — A. Gallerani, M. Muzzarelli, A. Ferrari, S. Cattini, L. Rovati, ACTA IMEKO, 2026.
  • Towards the monitoring of dialysis treatment through absorption and endogenous fluorescence techniques — V. D. Pinto et al., Measurement Sensors, 2024.
  • DermaBridge: A Novel Mixed-Signal System for Electrodermal Activity Measurements With Dynamically Controlled AC Excitation — M. Micolitti et al., IEEE Sensors Journal, 2025.

Spectral, photometric and radiometric measurement

How to get trustworthy numbers out of light. This area covers calibration methods that make compact, low-cost multi-channel sensors usable as photometric and radiometric instruments, near-infrared spectroscopy for in-field estimation of crop moisture and density, photoacoustic gas detection with the optical excitation tailored to the acoustic mode structure of the resonant cell, and the characterization of the optical components themselves — including high-reflectance diffuse coatings for 3D-printed optics.

Selected work

  • Flexible calibration method: Photometric and radiometric measurements with compact low-cost multi-channel sensors — A. Besozzi, G. Gibertoni, L. Rovati, A. Gibaldi, ACTA IMEKO, 2026.
  • Photoacoustic Gas Detection: Optical Excitation Tailored to Acoustic Mode Structure of the Resonant Cell — G. Gibertoni, D. Goldoni, A. Fort, E. Panzardi, M. Mugnaini, L. Rovati, IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement, 2026.
  • Design and Performance of a Near-Infrared Spectroscopy Measurement System for In-Field Alfalfa Moisture Measurement — G. Gibertoni, N. Lenzini, L. Ferrari, L. Rovati, Photonics, 2022.
  • Partial Least Squares Estimation of Crop Moisture and Density by Near-Infrared Spectroscopy — D. Cassanelli, N. Lenzini, L. Ferrari, L. Rovati, IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement, 2021.
  • Characterization of high-reflectance diffuse coatings for 3D-printed optics — G. Gibertoni, L. Rovati, ACTA IMEKO, 2026.

Automotive optical sensing, LiDAR metrology and driver state

Measurement science applied to vehicles and the road. The group has built a sustained body of work on how to benchmark 3D LiDAR — object-detection probability, lateral resolution, systematic errors, artefacts introduced during in-motion measurement, and degradation in fog — on testing the safety of the optical radiation these scanners emit, and on how visibility should be quantified so that the figure represents what a human driver, or a camera-based ADAS, actually perceives. A parallel line infers driver state from wearable physiological signals.

Selected work

  • A Method for Estimating Object Detection Probability, Lateral Resolution, and Errors in 3-D LiDARs — S. Cattini, D. Cassanelli, L. Ferrari, L. Rovati, IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement, 2023.
  • A Simplified Method to Test the Safety of the Optical Radiations Emitted by 3D Scanning-LiDARs — S. Cattini, D. Cassanelli, A. Bertacchini, L. Rovati, IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement, 2025.
  • A simple experimental method to estimate and benchmark automotive LIDARs performance in fog — D. Cassanelli, S. Cattini, L. Medici, L. Ferrari, L. Rovati, ACTA IMEKO, 2024.
  • On the effectiveness of the Meteorological Optical Range for representing visibility as perceived by human drivers and by ADAS employing visible cameras — D. Cassanelli, S. Cattini, L. Rovati, ACTA IMEKO, 2026.
  • Driver Drowsiness Detection: A Machine Learning Approach on Skin Conductance — A. Amidei et al., Sensors, 2023.

Nanoscale and electrochemical sensing for biological and environmental analysis

Sensing below the optical scale. Together with the nanoelectronics group, the laboratory works on nanoelectrode-array biosensors read out by high-frequency impedance and capacitance spectroscopy: multispectral capacitance imaging of single red blood cells, size-resolved detection of nano- and micro-plastics in water at varying salinity, and localization of analytes above the sensor surface. Physics-based simulation, machine learning and Bayesian inference are combined to turn the raw electrical response into a metrologically defensible measurement.

Selected work

  • Toward Continuous Nano-Plastic Monitoring in Water by High Frequency Impedance Measurement With Nano-Electrode Arrays — D. Goldoni, L. Rovati, L. Selmi, IEEE Sensors Journal, 2023.
  • Multispectral Capacitance Imaging of Single Red Blood Cells: Experiments and Simulations — D. Goldoni, J. Nicolini, L. Rovati, L. Selmi, IEEE SENSORS, 2025.
  • Integrating physics-based simulations, machine learning, and Bayesian inference for accurate detection and metrology of elongated nanoscale analytes — E. Khodadadian et al., Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, 2025.
  • Machine learning and data augmentation methods for multispectral capacitance images of nanoparticles with nanoelectrodes array biosensors — F. Lombardo, F. Pittino, D. Goldoni, L. Selmi, Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, 2023.
  • Recent Advances in Self-Powered Electrochemical Biosensors for Early Diagnosis of Diseases — V. Galstyan et al., Advanced Materials Technologies, 2024.

Facilities & capabilities

OptoLAB supports the development of complete optical measurement chains, from experimental layouts and optomechanics to electronic acquisition, calibration and computational analysis.

Optical prototyping

Experimental layouts for illumination, collection, imaging and spectral sensing, supported by C-mount optics, broadband mirrors, filters and optomechanical components.

Imaging sensors

Machine-vision cameras for experimental image acquisition, including Matrix Vision/Balluff BlueFOX3 USB3 camera systems in 3 MP and 5 MP configurations.

Electronic instrumentation

Sensor interfaces, acquisition electronics and optoelectronic system integration for weak-signal and multi-channel measurements.

Embedded platforms

Compact computing and embedded-acquisition systems for device control, communication, portable instrumentation and edge processing.

Calibration workflows

Methods and software for sensor characterization, reference comparison, photometric and radiometric calibration and model fitting.

Computational prototyping

Dedicated computing resources and in-house additive manufacturing for data analysis, instrument housings, optical supports and test parts.
